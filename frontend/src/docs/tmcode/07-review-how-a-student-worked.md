Use this guide when you want to know how a piece of work was made: what the student ran, what they pasted in, and where AI helped.

## What TMCode records

TMCode keeps an Activity log of work on an assignment. It records everything except typing:

- files created, renamed, moved, copied and deleted;
- copy, cut and paste, with where the pasted text came from;
- runs, tests and terminal commands;
- saves and submits to Task Mentor;
- every use of AI, with the tool named, and attempts to use AI that is switched off;
- time spent in other apps, when it is 30 seconds or more.

## Where to read it

- **In Task Mentor:** for work linked to Task Mentor, the log goes there. Open the student's submission in grading and open its **Activity** tab. It sums up AI use and pastes.
- **In TMCode:** the **Activity** tab in the bottom panel shows the log on that computer. Students see their own log there.

## Read the log in TMCode

1. Open the **Activity** tab in the bottom panel.
2. Read the day's header. It sums up the day: file changes, runs, pastes, time in other apps and AI use.
3. Use the filters to narrow the list: **All**, **AI**, **Files**, **Copy & paste**, **Runs & terminal**, **Projects & git** and **Extensions**.
4. Type in **Filter activity** to search.
5. Click an entry to see its details in plain words.

## What the entries mean

| Entry | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Copied inside TMCode" | The student moved their own text from one place to another |
| "From an AI's answer" | The pasted text came from an AI's answer in TMCode |
| "From outside TMCode" | The pasted text came from another app or a website |
| "Away from TMCode for 3 min" | The student was in another app for that long |

> **Important:** The log shows what happened, not why. A paste from outside can be the student's own notes. Time away can be reading your brief in a browser. Use the log to start a conversation, not as proof on its own.

## A fair way to follow up

1. Look for pastes from outside or from an AI.
2. Compare with the AI setting you chose for the work.
3. Ask the student to explain the code in question. In TMCode, **Explain-Your-Code Questions (Grading)** writes questions for you from the student's own code.
