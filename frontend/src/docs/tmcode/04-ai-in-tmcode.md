---
title: How AI works in TMCode
summary: Know which AI help students can get, who decides it, and what students see when AI is off.
---

TMCode has a built-in assistant called TMCode AI. This guide explains how much help it gives students, so you can choose the right setting for each piece of work.

## Who decides

TMCode follows Task Mentor's AI setting. That setting combines three things:

- the school's AI switch;
- your choice for the assignment or quiz, which you make in Task Mentor;
- the person's role.

A change reaches TMCode without a restart.

## The three levels

A badge in the status bar, at the bottom of the window, shows a person's level. Pointing at it shows why.

| Badge | What the student gets |
|---|---|
| **Exam** or **No AI** | No AI panel, no AI in the terminal, no suggestions |
| **Learning** | A tutor that explains and asks questions. It reads the project but changes nothing, and it answers without code |
| **Industrial · Guided** | The full assistant. It can write code, and asks the student to explain each change before they accept it |

Staff normally work in **Industrial · Open**, the full assistant without the student steps.

A student can lower their own level for practice, for example with **Switch to Learning for This Project**.

## In exams

- TMCode AI is off during exams, unless you allowed AI for that quiz in Task Mentor.
- Other AI tools, such as AI extensions and AI programs in the terminal, never run during exams.

## What students see when AI is off

The AI panel says why, in one line. For example:

- "Your school has turned AI off."
- "Your teacher turned AI off for this assignment."

## What is recorded

Every use of AI is written to the student's Activity log, with the AI tool named. Attempts to use an AI tool that is switched off are recorded too. When a student submits, TMCode shows them a summary of how AI was used on the assignment, as you will see it.

> **Important:** The block on other AI tools runs on the student's computer and is best-effort. A renamed program can get past it. Do not rely on the block alone.

## If something goes wrong

- **A student says AI is off but you allowed it.** Ask them to point at the badge in the status bar. It names the reason: the school, the assignment, or their own choice. If it was their own choice, they choose **Back to What's Set for You**.
